You’d think my wife’s favorite person in the entire world would be me. Well, it’s not. It’s Harry Styles. I can’t even complain.

So, naturally, when he comes to our city I get her tickets for her birthday.

This project is the result of me trying to figure out how to do something less lame than just showing her the StubHub receipt.

In total this took about 117 prompts over the course of 24h using Opus 5.5 mostly, all through bb. I do a lot of prompting these days especially creatively so I figured I’d show a bit about my process.

## It started as a print job

It actually started as a print job. I thought ok it might be super cool to print some physical tickets and put them in a gift bag.

I make a lot of personal software and one of those is a local image-generator aggregate CLI I called “imgen” — so we generated a bunch of options: a night-sky stub, a kiss clock, an old-school admit-one, a mirror-ball ticket, letterpress.

<Widget name="tickets" />

Then I got excited (as one does when creativity starts flowing) and the scope grew.

“What if there was a QR code in the gift bag that opened a little 3D treasure hunt?”

In a few minutes we had a prototype: Set a clock to 7:45, spin a disco ball, find three hidden kisses, tear open a foil packet to get the tickets.

<Strip images={[["play/0-intro.jpg", "Intro screen with her name"], ["play/1-clock.jpg", "Set the clock to 7:45"], ["play/3-disco.jpg", "Spin the ball"], ["play/4-kisses.jpg", "Find three kisses"], ["play/7-tearing.jpg", "Tearing it open"], ["play/9-finale.jpg", "The tickets"]]} />

It worked, and it was kind of clever, but it was asking too much. I wanted interactivity but it was a little too surreal and wasn’t immediately clicking. Plus puzzles just get between her and the moment. So I asked for something way simpler:

> she scans it, it opens up, there's a quick loading screen, and then it's just like … happy birthday babe, you're the best thing that's ever happened to me … you have until April 2 to find a babysitter

One card per tap. This was it, we were onto something.

## Go look at the real thing

Then it needed to look like his tour. I asked for some real recon: what's he actually putting on the screens? Written reviews say basically nothing about the visuals, so the agent pulled a frame every minute out of a fan's 4K video of a full stadium show, and we made a mood board out of what was on the LED wall.

<Board images={[["tour/scanlines.jpg", "Pink scanlines"], ["tour/bullseye.jpg", "Heat-map bullseye"], ["tour/triangles.jpg", "Mirror triangles"], ["tour/rainbow.jpg", "Rainbow bands"]]} credit="Frames from SandraOnTour's video of the tour." />

Those four became the show's LED wall, running live in three.js on WebGPU: pink scanlines with little hand-drawn marks, a posterized bullseye, flipping triangles, rainbow bands.

The disco balls weren't even my idea. I'd sent the StubHub receipt in my very first prompt, and our seats are on the floor in a section called "DISCO" (his album is literally called *Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally.*). So the agent put a mirror ball on the very first tickets, I loved it, and we leaned all the way in. They ended up in every scene. The only thing they really needed was a proper reflection map so they had something to reflect.

## React to what comes back

Most of the build was just me watching it on my phone and saying what felt off.

> I wonder if there is more interesting distinction between the scenes, so each slide right now just kind of changes camera and changes the background and keeps the disco balls

So each card turned into its own spot at a concert: a lone ball in the dark, the party, inside the ball, facing the crowd, a tunnel of screens. A quick white flash between cards hides the camera jump.

<Strip images={[["versions/v3-1-intimate.jpg", "Hey babe, close and dim"], ["versions/v3-2-party.jpg", "Happy birthday, the party"], ["versions/v3-3-inside.jpg", "Best thing, inside the light"], ["versions/v3-4-crowd.jpg", "Return the favor, the stars"], ["versions/v3-5-tunnel.jpg", "Babysitter, the tunnel"]]} />

The crowd scene took a few rounds. First it was glowing dots, then glowing dots doing a wave, and none of it read as a crowd. My exact note was "still don't like the crowd looks very vibe coded lol". What finally worked was a photoreal stadium shot (generated, from the stage looking out) with live phone lights twinkling on top, and a real applause recording that swells as the house lights come up.

<Widget name="crowd" />

For music, I really wanted an actual Harry Styles song, which obviously wasn't happening. So I asked for Suno, and we made an instrumental called "For Nathalie," plus a second track for the finale that opens on a huge hit.

Then the agent did something I loved: it made the song sound like it's coming from somewhere. Muffled through a wall, then from the next room, then underwater inside the ball, then right there at the party. I asked for even more of it ("super muffled on the hey babe… even more muffled and low"). You don't really notice it. You just kind of feel where you are.

## The ideas only you'll have

Then I had my favorite idea of the whole thing. A surprise hits way harder when she thinks she already knows what she's getting:

> i want to … do a version where we do a super cheesy slide show of our love and then after like 10s it glitches and fades to black then our hey babe shows on the black

The first pass was cute but too tame, and the music was made in the browser. My note: "MORE cheesy and real sfx and music nothing procedural." So now it opens on the most gloriously terrible "Our Love Story" slideshow (Pachelbel's Canon, sparkles, star wipes, 3D flips, every photo flying in), then a record scratch, a glitch, black, and "Hey babe." The next card calls it out: *"You didn't really think all I got you was a cheesy slideshow, did you?"*

It's her first birthday as a mom, so I sent over a few more photos for that too.

## When words run out, make a tool

The finale is where the tickets show up, so it had to land. I can hear the exact moment in the song where it should hit, but I can't describe it. So I asked for the finale track on a little player I could scrub, and just sent back screenshots like "ok the drum run starts here."

After that we were talking in timestamps. The burst went from 0:24.25 to 0:23.73 ("the drums need to end at the explosion") to "almost exactly 0:23," and the song ended up starting at 0:18.40.

The flight into the finale took the most back and forth. I'm not a motion designer, so mostly I just described it:

> immediately when we start the finale buildup i want to see MOVEMENT like the tunnel starts spinning we fly through it we fly through an asteroid field of pictures and disco balls and then the pillar and the big one settle in and explode

Then a lot of "still feel the linear easing," "stuttery," "a little less dizzying." The one that finally cracked it: "you lengthen the fly by adding more SPACE not faking it with time delays." So the flight got longer by actually flying farther, past more photos and balls, at full speed.

<Widget name="flight" />

From her seat the flight is just speed, so for this post we went back and filmed it from the outside: a debug camera that follows her camera around, plus a fake clock that steps one frame at a time so every frame renders perfectly. Switch the angles and it stays in sync.

<Widget name="cameras" />

## The phone is always right

A dropped frame in the middle of a surprise feels like a bug, so I kept saying "hitches" until they were gone.

Most of them came from the GPU compiling something the first time it's drawn. We added a probe that logged every compile and where in the show it happened, then drew everything once behind the loading screen before she ever taps. My favorite sneaky one: a light was attached to a disco ball, so hiding the ball removed the light, and changing the light count made every material recompile. Now that light just stays on at zero brightness.

<Widget name="hitch" />

Then there was the bug that almost broke me. The agent was testing in desktop Chrome, the logs were clean, and it kept telling me the stutter wasn't there. It was, only on an iPhone.

> this is terrible i need to give this to her i can't keep repeating the same issues and you telling me they don't exist

## She loved it (mostly)

> ok she loved it!! there are a few images she wants to replace hahaha

Of course she did. So we made her a page with every photo in the show and a Replace button on each one. Pick a photo, tap the faces, and it gets cropped into the same frame everywhere it shows up.

She also hit the stutter, and sometimes on a rewatch the flight skipped entirely. So we added a tiny endpoint so my phone could send the song's position to my laptop every frame, and the first run had the answer: at the cut, the finale was **already 2.2 seconds in**.

iOS needs a tap to unlock audio, so on her first tap we `play()` the finale and immediately `pause()` it. On iOS the pause doesn't always take, and the song just keeps playing silently in the background.

<Widget name="race" />

The fix: the finale gets stopped and rewound once it's actually playing, and rewound again right before it's due. And every visual follows the song's playhead instead of a timer, so if the audio's late, the burst is late with it and still lands on the hit.

And the link preview, for when I texted it to her: our photos drifting by as polaroids.

## What I used

- **Claude Code** (Opus 5.5) through **bb**, start to finish. 118 prompts and 79 commits.
- **imgen** for the first round of tickets.
- **three.js on WebGPU** for the 3D, **gsap** for the cards, **Vite** and **Vercel** to ship it.
- **Suno** for both songs, plus real recordings for the applause, the slideshow and the sound effects.
- **A muted headless browser** for screenshots and profiling, and **my actual iPhone** for the truth.
- **A bunch of throwaway tools** we made along the way: the scrubber, the compile probe, the phone logging endpoint, the photo replacer, and the debug camera.

Looking back, my job was mostly taste. The agent builds faster than I can react. What it can't do is know my wife. The puzzles getting cut, the fake slideshow, the exact drum hit: that part was me.

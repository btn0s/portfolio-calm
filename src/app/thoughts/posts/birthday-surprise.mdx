For my wife's birthday I bought us tickets to see Harry Styles. They only existed in an app, and forwarding her the confirmation email seemed like a pretty lame way to reveal a surprise. So I figured why not put my skills to good use and make something cool.

What I ended up with is a little show she opens from a QR code. Mirror balls, an LED wall, our photos, the whole thing.

I built it over a weekend with Claude Code. So here's a peek into my process: how I pull together creative projects using these new superpowers we call agents, what my stack looks like on a project like this, and how we made sure a 3D show actually ran smooth on her phone.

## How I work with an agent

Honestly it's pretty simple. I talk, it builds, I react. I never wrote a spec. I just described what I wanted the way I'd describe it to a friend, looked at what came back, and told it what felt off.

The thing that changes is what my job is. The agent can build three versions before I've finished my coffee. What it can't do is know my wife. So most of my time went to three things: making the big calls, saying exactly how something felt, and checking it on the real thing.

## Go wide, then cut

It actually started as a print job. I figured I'd print some physical tickets to hand her, so we generated a bunch of options: a night-sky stub, a kiss clock, an old-school admit-one, a mirror-ball ticket, letterpress.

Then I got excited and the scope grew. What if there was a QR code in the gift bag that opened a little 3D treasure hunt? Set a clock to 7:45, spin a disco ball, find three hidden kisses, tear open a foil packet to get the tickets.

## Steal from the real thing

If you ask an agent for "a concert," you get the average of every concert ever. It's generic. The fix is to hand it the real thing.

So we pulled frames from a fan's 4K video of the tour and made a mood board out of what was on the screens.

<Board images={[["tour/scanlines.jpg", "Pink scanlines"], ["tour/bullseye.jpg", "Heat-map bullseye"], ["tour/triangles.jpg", "Mirror triangles"], ["tour/rainbow.jpg", "Rainbow bands"]]} credit="Frames from SandraOnTour's video of the tour." />

Those four became the show's LED wall, running live in three.js on WebGPU: pink scanlines with little hand-drawn marks that boil a few times a second, a posterized bullseye, flipping triangles, rainbow bands.

The crowd scene took four tries. Glowing dots doing a wave, silhouettes holding up phones, none of it worked. Anything drawn just looked like a drawing of a crowd. My exact note was "the crowd looks very vibe coded" lol. What finally worked was a real stadium photo with live phone lights on top, and a real applause recording that swells as the house lights come up.

<Widget name="crowd" />

Same deal with sound. I told it "real sfx and music, nothing procedural." Using an actual Harry Styles song wasn't an option, so I made an instrumental in Suno and called it "For Nathalie." A second Suno track became the finale, mostly because it opens with a huge hit. Everything else is licensed and credited: stadium noise, applause, a record scratch.

## The details that sell it

Usually there's one detail carrying the whole effect. Find it and spend your time there.

The mirror balls are a good example. They're real geometry, rows of tiny flat tiles, and each tile is tilted a few degrees off. That little wobble *is* the disco ball. A perfect sphere just looks like chrome. Drag the slider and you'll see what I mean.

<Widget name="ball" />

Early on, every card just moved the camera and swapped the background, and it all kind of blurred together. I asked for "more interesting distinction between the scenes," and each card turned into its own spot at a concert: a lone ball in the dark, the party, inside the ball, facing the crowd, a tunnel of screens. A quick white flash between cards hides the camera jump.

<Strip images={[["versions/v3-1-intimate.jpg", "Hey babe, close and dim"], ["versions/v3-2-party.jpg", "Happy birthday, the party"], ["versions/v3-3-inside.jpg", "Best thing, inside the light"], ["versions/v3-4-crowd.jpg", "Return the favor, the stars"], ["versions/v3-5-tunnel.jpg", "Babysitter, the tunnel"]]} />

The sound moves around too. One song plays through the whole thing, and a low-pass filter makes it sound like it's coming through a wall, then from the next room, then inside the ball, then right there at the party. You don't really notice it. You just kind of feel where you are.

## Setting up the surprise

A surprise hits way harder when she thinks she already knows what she's getting. This was my favorite idea of the whole project:

> i want to … do a version where we do a super cheesy slide show of our love and then after like 10s it glitches and fades to black then our hey babe shows on the black

So it opens on the most gloriously terrible "Our Love Story" slideshow. Pachelbel's Canon, sparkles, a cube turn, a film strip, every photo flying into a heart. Then a record scratch, a glitch, black, and "Hey babe." The next card calls it out: *"You didn't really think all I got you was a cheesy slideshow, did you?"*

No agent was ever going to come up with that one. That came from knowing her.

## Build tools to talk to it

I can hear the exact moment in a song where the finale should hit. I can't describe it in words though. So I asked for the finale track on a little player I could scrub, and just sent back screenshots like "ok the drum run starts here." After that we were talking in timestamps. The burst went from 0:24.25 to 0:23.73 to 0:23.00, and the start landed on 0:18.40.

<Strip images={[["versions/fin-a-2-ball-drop.jpg", "A giant ball drops"], ["versions/fin-b-2-panels-fly.jpg", "Photo panels fly past"], ["versions/fin-c-2-column.jpg", "A column of photos and balls"], ["versions/fin-c-3-tickets.jpg", "The tickets land"]]} />

I'm not a motion designer, and I told it that. What I could do was say exactly how it felt: robotic, slow, stuttery, dizzying. Each one of those words turned into one specific change to one curve, the camera's speed over the song. The note that finally cracked it was "you lengthen the fly by adding more SPACE not faking it with time delays."

<Widget name="flight" />

From her seat the flight is just speed, so I wanted to see it from the outside too. We added a debug camera that films the show while her camera keeps flying, plus a clock we can step one frame at a time so every frame renders perfectly. Switch the angles and it stays in sync.

## Making it smooth

A dropped frame in the middle of a surprise feels like a bug. It totally breaks the spell. So performance got just as much attention as the visuals.

Most of our hitches came from one thing. The first time a GPU draws a new kind of object, it has to compile a program for it, and that can freeze a frame. Claude wrote a little profiler that logged every compile, what was being drawn, and where in the show it happened. The fix was to draw everything once, hidden behind the loading screen, before she ever taps. My favorite sneaky one: turning a light off changed the light count, which made every material recompile. So now that light just stays on at zero brightness.

<Widget name="hitch" />

Then there was the bug that almost broke me. Claude was testing in desktop Chrome, the logs were clean, and it kept telling me the stutter wasn't there. It was. Only on an iPhone though. So we added a tiny endpoint so my phone could send the song's position to my laptop every frame, and the very first run had the answer: at the cut, the finale was **already 2.2 seconds in**.

To unlock audio, iOS needs a tap, so on the first tap we `play()` the finale and immediately `pause()` it. Turns out on iOS the `play()` promise can resolve before playback actually starts. So the pause lands too early, and the song just keeps playing silently in the background.

<Widget name="race" />

The fix had two parts. When the finale fires its `playing` event, it gets stopped and rewound, and right before it's due we rewind it again just in case. And every visual follows the song's playhead instead of a timer, so if the audio's late, the burst is late with it and still lands on the hit.

## Handing it over

> ok she loved it!! there are a few images she wants to replace hahaha

Of course she did. So the last thing we built was for her: a page with every photo in the show and a Replace button on each one. Pick a photo, tap the faces, and it gets cropped to the same frame with the same white border everywhere it shows up. We rebuilt the link preview with her picks too, our photos drifting by as polaroids.

## My stack for this one

- **Claude Code** for basically everything, start to finish. About 120 prompts and 80 commits.
- **Local image generation** for the first round of ticket designs.
- **three.js on WebGPU** for the 3D, **gsap** for the cards, **Vite** and **Vercel** to build and ship it.
- **Suno** for both songs, plus licensed recordings for the crowd and sound effects.
- **A headless, muted browser** for screenshots and profiling, and **my actual iPhone** for the truth.
- **A bunch of little throwaway tools** we built along the way: the scrubber, the profiler, the phone logging endpoint, the photo replacer, and the debug camera.

## If you're going to try this

**Make the big calls yourself.** Cutting the puzzles, opening on a fake slideshow, making the flight longer instead of slower. The agent built fast, but every turn that mattered came from knowing who it was for.

**Talk in feelings first, then in numbers.** Start with how it feels. When words stop working, build a little tool that turns the feeling into something exact.

**Trust the device, not the logs.** The agent will sound super confident. The phone is always right.
